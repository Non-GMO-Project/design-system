# Non-GMO Project Verified trademark usage

**Program:** Non-GMO Project
**Brand context:** `data-brand="nongmo"`
**Source:** *Non-GMO Project Trademark Use Guide* v2.2. Where this page and the guide differ, the guide wins.
**For:** Participants with a signed license agreement and Non-GMO Project Verified products. Also covers retailers, wholesalers and industry partners.
**Contact:** Client Experience Team, verification@nongmoproject.org
**Page:** `trademark/non-gmo-project.html`

How to use the Non-GMO Project Verified Mark and the other Non-GMO Project trademarks on packaging, websites and marketing. The Mark tells shoppers that a product has met the Non-GMO Project Standard. Using it correctly keeps that promise clear.

## Before you start

- **Only verified products carry the Mark.** The Product Verification Program verifies individual products, not brands or companies. Every product shown or named next to the Mark must be Non-GMO Project Verified.
- **You need a signed license agreement.** Using the trademarks on products or in marketing (ads, website listings, social media) is limited to licensees who have signed an agreement with the Non-GMO Project. Using them without one is trademark infringement and a breach of the license agreement.
- **Never use the Mark before verification.** Showing it on packaging or marketing before a product is verified is strictly prohibited.
- **Ask when in doubt.** Send questions about packaging or any other use to verification@nongmoproject.org for approval.

## Specifications

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Full color first | The full-color Mark is the most recognized and is recommended |
| Single color | If full-color printing is a problem, use the single-color Mark with its keyline, in black or white only |
| White is part of the Mark | When printing the full-color Mark on a colored or clear substrate, print the white as a spot color. Never let the background show through the full-color Mark |
| URL | The URL at the bottom of the Mark is required and must stay readable |
| Clear space | Keep a space equal to the height of the capital "N" in "Non-GMO" on every side, measured from the white edge. No text or graphic may touch the Mark or enter that space. The space scales with the Mark |
| Minimum size, English and French | 3/8 inch tall and 1/2 inch wide |
| Minimum size, bilingual | 3/8 inch tall and 3/4 inch wide |
| Minimum size, on screen | Large enough that the URL can be read. `TODO(brand): the guide sets print sizes only. The design system uses 72px wide on screen until the program confirms a digital minimum.` |
| Brand hierarchy | The Mark must never look like the name of your business or product. Keep it smaller than your own logo and lower in the layout |

## Incorrect uses

These are the most common mistakes. The list is not complete; if a use is not covered here, ask first.

- Don't add gradients, drop shadows or glow effects.
- Don't remove the URL.
- Don't change the colors.
- Don't place the full-color Mark on a transparent background. The white must be there.
- Don't squish, stretch or rotate the Mark.
- Don't use a low-resolution or low-quality file.
- Don't remove the keyline from a single-color Mark.
- Don't add a decorative border.
- Don't recreate or stylize "Non-GMO Project" or "Non-GMO Project Verified". Write these claims in plain text only.
- Don't crop the Mark or use only part of it.
- Don't use a crossed-out "GMO" symbol. It implies a complete absence claim, which the Non-GMO Project Standard does not allow.

## Type with the Mark

When you add text next to the Mark, use the Non-GMO Project typefaces:

- **Avenir** (Book, Book Oblique, Black) is the Mark's primary font. Use it for promotional text next to the Mark.
- **Lora** (Regular, Italic, Bold, Medium Italic) is for body copy and the tagline *Look for the Butterfly*.

Both are licensed for personal and commercial use through Adobe Fonts and Google Fonts. The Non-GMO Project cannot transfer fonts to you; you are responsible for your own font licenses.

## Text next to the Mark

You may add text that reminds shoppers to look for the Mark. Acceptable examples:

- "Look for the Butterfly"
- "See our full list of Non-GMO Project Verified products at www.nongmoproject.org"
- "X number of our products are now Non-GMO Project Verified"

## Marketing and websites

Brand hierarchy keeps your materials compliant. The Mark is a product certification and a registered trademark, so:

- It must always be less prominent than everything else in the piece.
- It must never be mistaken for another company's or organization's identity.
- It can be smaller than other logos and placed lower in the layout.
- It must never suggest that the Non-GMO Project endorses anything other than the verified products.

Good ways to show verification online:

- Show the Mark next to a list of all your verified products.
- Show the Mark next to other certifications, with a note that each product is evaluated separately.
- Create a page about your commitment to ingredient transparency and non-GMO products.

The Mark can also appear on ads, sell sheets, in-store signage, coupons, point-of-purchase materials, trade show displays and social media posts, as long as they are about verified products.

**Not allowed on company stationery.** The Mark refers to products, not companies, so it never goes on letterhead, fax covers, email signatures, business cards, folders, envelopes or memo cards.

Two exceptions:

- **Company reports:** the Mark may appear on the pages that refer to Non-GMO Project Verified products, and only those pages.
- **Wholesale supplier email signatures:** allowed if Non-GMO Project verification applies to your entire product line.

## Accurate and truthful claims

Labels on verified products must be accurate, truthful and not misleading.

| Claim | Rule |
|---|---|
| In process | Never promote "in process" status on packaging or marketing. You may talk about working toward verification in conversation, but never next to the Mark |
| GMO free | Not allowed, nor are similar claims such as "zero GMOs" or "100% no GMOs". They are not legally or scientifically defensible. The Non-GMO Project claim is a true statement that acknowledges contamination risk while assuring shoppers the product complies with the Standard |
| First and only | Not allowed, for example "first Non-GMO Project Verified potato chip" or "only Non-GMO Project Verified pasta". With many technical administrators verifying thousands of products, these claims cannot be confirmed |
| Competing certifications | No other non-GMO certification may appear with the Non-GMO Project trademarks. Plain text such as "non-GMO" that is not tied to another organization is fine |
| Bioengineered food disclosure | Never use the trademarks on any product, display or material that also carries a bioengineered food disclosure under the National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard (NBFDS) |
