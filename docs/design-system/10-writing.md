# Writing and copy

How the Food Integrity Project and its programs sound, in interfaces, on public pages, in email and in print. One set of writing rules for everyone; each brand adds its own voice on top. Sources: the *Writer's Guide* (Sixth Edition, September 2025) for audiences, terms, grammar and formatting; the *Food Integrity Project Style Guide* (voice lines, written reference); and the earlier program guides (voice attributes, claims guidance). The Writer's Guide also lists fonts and colors per brand. For those, this system follows the Food Integrity Project Style Guide (see `02-color.md` and `03-typography.md`).

## Audiences

Name the audience before writing. It sets the reading level, the style guide and some terms (see "GMO" in the word list).

| Audience | Who and what | Examples | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| General | Shoppers and the public. Consumer-facing material | Social media, blog posts, fact pages, take-action pages | AP Style |
| Business | Brands and retailers, business to business. Professional and collegial, for readers who are not experts on GMOs or UPFs | Participant portal, trade show material, sales email | AP Style |
| Technical | Research-oriented readers | The Standards, material for Technical Administrators and labs, some verification guides | Chicago Manual of Style |

## Principles

1. **Plain first.** Write for a reader who is busy, not an expert. Aim for an 8th to 10th grade reading level (Flesch-Kincaid) on public pages. Short sentences, common words, one idea per paragraph.
2. **Precise, not vague.** Name the product, the date, the standard, the number. "Verification expires December 1, 2026" beats "Your status may change soon."
3. **Evidence over adjectives.** Say what was tested, by whom, against what. Cite sources. Let facts carry the conviction.
4. **Calm, never alarmed.** No fear-based language about GMOs, processed food or the people who eat it. State standards and facts.
5. **Helpful at the hard moments.** Errors, denials and lapses say what happened, why, and what to do next, in that order.
6. **The same word for the same thing.** Pick one term and keep it through a whole flow, product and program.
7. **Clear, trustworthy and inclusive.** Keep that tone across every brand and channel.
8. **A second reader.** Before anything public-facing is published or posted, at least one other person reads it. The Marketing and Communications team reviews public-facing content for proofing and audience fit.

**References:** AP Stylebook for public material (consumer, retailer and marketing); Chicago Manual of Style for technical writing (the Standards, research reports); Merriam-Webster for spelling and definitions. Where this page and AP Style differ, this page wins.

## Voice by brand

The voice is the personality; it stays the same. Tone (below) shifts with the situation.

| Brand | Voice | Sounds like | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food Integrity Project | Institutional, plain, unhurried | An organization that checks its work and explains its reasoning. Uses "we" | Hype, urgency, making verification claims (those belong to the programs) |
| Non-GMO Project | Confident, established, shopper-facing | A trusted guide who knows the Standard inside out and explains it warmly | Fear about GMOs, jargon without explanation, "GMO-free" |
| Non-UPF Verified | Direct, current, standard-setting | Someone who reads ingredient lists and cooks dinner. Practical and concrete | Moralizing, shaming processed food or the people who eat it, exclusivity claims |
| Food Integrity Collective | Collegial, editorial, member-facing | A colleague inviting you into shared work. Uses "you" and "together" | Shopper marketing tone, vague calls to "join the movement" without saying what joining means |

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome and onboarding | Warm, specific about what comes next | "Welcome, Brightfield Mills. Start by adding the products you want verified. It takes about 10 minutes per product." |
| Success | Brief, past tense, no fanfare | "Product submitted for review." |
| Verification granted | Proud but plain. This is the one moment that may celebrate | "Heritage Rolled Oats is Non-GMO Project Verified. You can now use the verification mark on pack." |
| Verification denied or not eligible | Respectful, factual, with a path forward | "Cultured Oat Butter is not eligible yet. One ingredient, natural flavor, has no supplier documentation. Add it and we will review again." |
| Lapsed or expiring | Clear dates, no blame | "Verification for Sprouted Lentil Pasta expires December 1, 2026. Renew before then to keep the mark on pack." |
| Errors | Calm, specific, fixable. Never "Oops" | "We could not upload the spec sheet. The file is larger than 20 MB. Compress it or upload a PDF instead." |
| Waiting | Honest about time | "Most reviews finish in 4 to 6 weeks. We will email you at each step." |
| Fundraising | Grounded, specific about impact | "Your gift funds independent testing of 40 products this year." |
| Legal and policy | Plain summary first, then the full text | "In short: you may use the mark only on products that are Verified today." |

## UI copy patterns

| Pattern | Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary button | Submit for review | Submit | Says exactly what happens |
| Destructive confirm | Delete product | OK | The button repeats the action |
| Cancel in a confirm | Keep product | Cancel | Both choices are named for their outcome |
| Toast | Product submitted | Success! Your product has been submitted successfully. | Past tense of the action, nothing more |
| Field error | Enter the product name as it appears on pack. | Invalid input | Says how to fix it |
| Empty state | Products you submit for verification will appear here. | No data | Says what will appear and invites the action |
| Page title | Verified products | Product Verification Status Dashboard | Name things the way people do |
| Link text | Read the Non-GMO Project Standard | Click here | Link text makes sense out of context |
| Help text | 12 digits, no spaces. | Please ensure that the UPC you enter is formatted correctly. | Example of the format, not a warning |
| Loading | Loading products | Please wait... | Names what is loading |
| Status label | Expires December 1, 2026 | Expiring soon!! | A date is more useful than urgency |
| Event time | December 3 at 6 pm Pacific | 12/3 @ 6:00 PM PST | The date and time format from the grammar table |
| AI content | AI summary · Check against the source document | (no label) | AI-generated content is always disclosed |

## Names and claims

- Write program names exactly: **Non-GMO Project**, **Non-UPF Verified**, **Food Integrity Collective**, **the Food Integrity Project**. Never "Non-GMO" alone as a program name, never "NGP" or "FIC" in public copy.
- First mention of a program: "Non-GMO Project, a program of the Food Integrity Project." After that, the program name alone, or "the Project" for the Non-GMO Project.
- Our trademarks include Non-GMO Project Verified, the Non-GMO Project logo, Non-GMO Project (lowercase "the" before it) and Look for the Butterfly. Capitalize them as written. Lowercase "trademarks" when talking about them in general. Don't add the ® symbol unless it is legally required. Mark use follows each program's trademark use guide (see `trademark/`).
- We verify products, not brands. Never say a product, brand or company is Verified unless the data says it is today. Company-wide claims only when every product is Verified.
- Non-UPF Verified: no exclusivity claims ("first", "only"); no "in process" claims on packaging or marketing; "UPF free" only when accurate, truthful and lawful.
- Never imply the parent organization verifies products. Claims belong to the programs.
- Support specific claims with a citation. On screen, a link to the source is usually enough. Use Chicago-style citations in print, for sources that are not online, and where links would get in the reader's way.
- Attribute every statistic with a source and year, in the pattern "[number]% of shoppers [finding] ([source], [year])." Never publish a figure without one. For custom reports and proprietary data, follow the producer's citation rules; if there are none, give the source, the report or data set name and the date: SPINS sales data, "Q1 Non-GMO Project Verified 12 and 52 weeks," 2025.

## Grammar and mechanics

| Topic | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Sentence case for headings, buttons, labels, tabs and menus. Eyebrows are the only uppercase text | Submit for review |
| Lists in a sentence | No serial (Oxford) comma | Non-GMO Project, Non-UPF Verified and Food Integrity Collective |
| Bulleted lists | End each bullet with a period when the items are full sentences. No periods for words or phrases. Keep one format through the whole list | |
| Numbers | Numerals for all numbers in UI; words for one to nine in long-form prose. Thousands separator always | 3 products · 1,284 products |
| Dates | Month spelled out, no ordinal, year when it is not the current year or could be unclear | December 1, 2026 · August 3 (not August 3rd) |
| Times | 12-hour clock with "am" and "pm", no periods. Drop ":00". Spell out the time zone. Join date and time with "at" | August 5, 2024 at 3 pm Pacific · 6 pm (not 6:00 pm) · Pacific (not PST or PDT) |
| Date ranges | Hyphen, no spaces | March 3-5 · March 3-May 1, 2025 · April 21, 2022-June 18, 2023 |
| Other ranges | "to" in prose, en dash only in tables and charts | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Percentages | Numeral and % sign | 72% |
| Abbreviations | Expand on first use on each page, then the short form | Technical Administrator (TA) |
| Compound modifiers | Hyphenate two words that together modify a noun | high-risk crop · third-party verification |
| Species names | Italicize the full scientific name; capitalize the first word only | *Beta vulgaris* |
| Contractions | Allowed, and encouraged on public pages. Avoid in legal and policy text | we'll, you're |
| Exclamation marks | Almost never. One is allowed in a Collective celebration or a "Verified" moment | Get Verified! |
| Ampersands | Only in proper names | Hive & Hollow |
| Person | "We" for the organization, "you" for the reader. "My" only for labels the person owns | Your products · My account |
| Quotation marks | Straight quotes in UI, curly quotes in print and long-form | |

## Inclusive language

- Write for everyone who eats. Avoid assumptions about diet, income, family or ability.
- Describe people with the words they use for themselves. Use "they" for a person whose pronouns are unknown.
- Never frame food choices as moral failures. Talk about systems and standards, not individual blame.
- Use examples, names and images that reflect the range of people who grow, make, sell and eat food.
- Avoid idioms that do not translate ("ballpark", "home run").

## Accessible copy

- Front-load the important words in headings, links and buttons.
- Never rely on direction or color alone: "the green button on the right" fails for many readers.
- Write alt text that says what matters in context. For a verified seal: "Non-GMO Project Verified".
- Keep instructions next to the field they belong to, not in a tooltip.
- Write error messages that make sense when read aloud on their own.

## Rules for Claude Code

- Identify the audience (general, business or technical) and the brand context before writing.
- Use the patterns and word list above for every string in a UI, email or page.
- Write buttons as verb + object, toasts in the past tense, errors as what happened + how to fix it.
- Use the brand voice from the table for the surface's brand context.
- Write dates and times in the format above: "December 3 at 6 pm Pacific". No serial comma.
- Never write "FIP", "NGP" or another program abbreviation in user-facing text.
- Never write a verification or health claim the data does not support. If unsure, ask and leave a `TODO(brand)`.
