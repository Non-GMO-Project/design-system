# Food Integrity Project

**Brand context:** `data-brand="fip"` (also the default)
**Role:** Parent organization and institutional voice. Governance, funding, policy, employer brand.
**Voice:** Institutional, plain, unhurried.

## Who it speaks to

Funders and donors, partners across the food system, press, policy audiences, staff, job seekers and anyone who works with more than one program.

## Personality

Rigorous, calm, connective. It sounds like an organization that checks its work and explains its reasoning. It does not shout. It is the ground its programs grow from, not a fourth seal.

## Color roles

| Role | Color | Hex | Token |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Birch | `#FFFDEB` | `--background` |
| Dark (all type, inverted panels) | Cacao | `#241A15` | `--foreground`, `--brand-dark` |
| Supporting tone | Loam | `#6B5644` | `--muted-foreground`; Loam 200 for `--brand-support` panels, with Cacao text |
| Signature | Milkweed Leaf | `#5F7A52` | `--primary`, `--brand-signature` |
| Utility | Frost | `#F3F4F2` | `--muted`, `--field` |

**Ratio:** 60 Birch, 24 Cacao type, 6 Loam, 10 Milkweed Leaf.

Green is the one hue no program owns, and Milkweed Leaf sits opposite Monarch orange. Cacao is a warm near-black that matches Forest and Dark Matter in weight; it sets type. The guide says Cacao "is never a fill" but also that each brand's dark "sets every inverted panel"; this system allows Cacao for inverted surfaces only (hero bands, footers, feature callouts and email header bands), never as a fill for controls or small elements.

## Logos

`fip-logo-*` and `fip-mark-*`. Parent mark in Cacao on Birch, or reversed to Birch on Cacao. The horizontal logo and the butterfly mark come from interim traces in dark gray `#35363A` (`*-mono-dark.svg`; source files are tracked as a `question` issue), and the build derives their Cacao (`color`) and Birch (`reversed`) versions. The stacked logo (`fip-logo-stacked-*`, for square and narrow formats) comes from the design team's source file, with Cacao and Birch versions derived by the build. On dark grounds use the Birch `fip-logo-horizontal-reversed.svg`, `fip-logo-stacked-reversed.svg` and `fip-mark-reversed.svg`. In an endorsement the parent appears as its butterfly beside the words "Food Integrity Project", in Cacao or Birch (see the lockups in `04`). See `../04-logos-and-marks.md`.

## Typography

Documents and standards pairing by default (Lora + Montserrat). Donor reports and the annual report use the editorial pairing (Lora + Quicksand).

## Imagery

Close, textured views of the natural systems food depends on (the style guide's butterfly-wing macro), and wide views of working landscapes and food systems: fields, processing lines, labs, people at work. Real people, real places, natural light. Avoid stock "hands holding a seedling" clichés and anything that looks staged.

**Photo library.** There is no single Food Integrity Project photo library; photos live in the team's own folders. When none fits, use the shared sources in `01-brand-architecture.md`, "Photography sourcing": the organization's Adobe Stock license, royalty-free sites, or a commissioned shoot where one is available.

## Voice

Plain, precise, evidence-first, unhurried. Names sources. Explains what a standard means in everyday terms. Uses "we" for the organization. Introduces programs in full on first mention: "Non-GMO Project, a program of the Food Integrity Project."

## Motion

Shared system. The signature moment, with a Milkweed Leaf glow, may mark the parent's own milestones, such as an event registration (see `../07-motion.md`).

## Typical surfaces

Organization website, annual report, donation flows (except campaigns for one program, which use that program's context throughout), careers, press, staff operating system, partner portal chrome, house-level "our programs" pages.

## Do

- Lead with clarity and evidence.
- Present the three programs as equals on house-level pages, each in its own brand context.
- Start every layout on Birch.

## Don't

- Override a program's own color in that program's spaces.
- Use the parent brand to make a verification claim. Claims belong to the programs and their seals.
- Abbreviate the name to "FIP" in anything people outside the organization read.
- Lock the parent mark to a seal or put them in one container.
